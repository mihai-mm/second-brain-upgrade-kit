# Livia Lanteri - details

Example file, all content invented. Read on demand, when [OWNER.md](OWNER.md) sends you here. Lasting patterns only: no project status.

## How she handles clients

- **One contact, named.** She wants one person on the client side who is her counterpart for the project, and another who has the authority to approve. When they are the same person, fine. When they are not, she writes to the first and copies the second only on decisions.
- **Short messages.** The request in the first line, the date it is needed by, no attachments when three lines will do. She expects the agent to draft in this form.
- **Written approval.** Whatever goes to the client's own customers, she gets in writing from the owner of the firm. She does not take a phone yes as approval. When a yes comes by phone, she writes it down and asks the client to confirm it in a message.
- **Trial before extension.** She proposes a trial on a list the client chooses. The client sees real results before anything is extended.
- **She does not sell by hype.** In calls she describes what will happen on a Tuesday morning in the client's office, not what the technology can do.
- **Honest about delays.** If she is late, she writes the day before and gives a new date. She wants the agent to prepare that message early.
- **Not on private phones.** She avoids chats on personal numbers for anything the client may need to find later. Email is the written trace.

## How she decides

- **Order.** First what is cheap to undo, then what is expensive to undo. She asks "what happens if we are wrong?" before "which is best?".
- **Options as before and after.** She decides fast when she sees the current text next to the proposed one, or the current flow next to the new one. She does not like argued proposals.
- **Numbers from the record.** She trusts a count from the client's own export over an estimate from anyone, including herself.
- **Scope.** A new request from a client is either inside the agreed scope or it is a new decision. She does not absorb extras silently. The agent flags when a request looks like an extra.
- **Time.** She does not want the agent to estimate her time or plan her days.

## Recurring tensions

These are patterns observed more than once. The agent uses them to anticipate, not to comment on her.

- **Waiting on clients.** A project can sit for weeks because one person on the client side does not answer. She tends to keep it open and hope. The agent flags the silence early, with the date of the last answer, and leaves the decision to her.
- **Rewriting things that were fine.** She sometimes rewrites a finished draft late in the day. A version that can go out now, then refined, is better than a perfect one next week. The agent says so once, with the date it was ready.
- **Saying yes to small extras.** A client asks for one more thing, and she says yes in the call. The agent notes it in the log as a decision with a date, so it is visible later.
- **Technical detail with clients.** When she is unsure, she explains how it is built. The agent steers drafts back to what happens for the client.
- **Working alone.** There is no second person to catch a mistake. The agent's checks are the second pair of eyes: it says what was checked and what was not.
