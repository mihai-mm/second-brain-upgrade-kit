# Discovery with open questions - the client's own words

Example file, all content invented. Pattern born in September 2026, preparing the first questions for a client. The questions listed Livia's own ideas as options ("is it mainly X or Y?"). The answers came back repeating her ideas, politely. She had learned nothing she did not already think.

## The pattern

When we prepare questions for a client (about a project, a website, a process):

1. **Open questions, in the client's words.** Collect what comes to mind for them. No closed options that limit ("pick at most two"). No examples or lists built on Livia's ideas.
2. **Livia's ideas are hypotheses.** They decide which questions to ask (one question per hypothesis, written separately, for Livia only). They are never written into the text the client receives. A question that proposes the answer gets it confirmed out of habit.
3. **Facts can be closed.** Who does a task, where a file is kept: options are fine, always with a free field.
4. **One thing per question.** The second half of a double question gets lost.
5. **Recent and concrete.** "The last time you chased an invoice, what did you do?" and not "does it happen?". A question with an easy exit ("none", "no") gets the easy exit.
6. **The client orders the ideas.** After the free answers, ask the client to put their own ideas in order of priority.
7. **Last question: "Is there anything else I should know?"** The richest answers often come there.

## Why it works

A client who is asked to choose between options chooses between our options. A client who is asked to remember a case describes their own. The first gives us confirmation, the second gives us information. The hypotheses are still useful: they tell us what to dig into by voice afterwards, when the client's answer is on the table.

## Rules of use

- Use the words the client used. Put them in quotation marks only when they said them.
- Do not give the project a name of our own in the questions. Use the word agreed with the client.
- Even when Livia says "start from the hypotheses", the text the client receives stays open. The hypotheses stay in her notes.
- Do not use it for a factual form with a known answer (a list of invoices, a booking export). It is for what we do not know yet.
- The mistake: an example in the introduction that anticipates an answer. Remove it, do not just flag it.

## Where it has been applied

- September 2026, first questions for the dental practice, rewritten from closed to open after the answers repeated the options.
- September 2026, the kickoff with the accounting firm, prepared the same way: three open questions about the last month's late payments, then a ranking by the people who answered.
