# Voice protocol

Use this every time a text will go out under [name of the person]. The goal is to say the thing the way they would say it to this reader. It is not to make the text more persuasive, polished or effective unless they ask.

## The seven steps

1. **Identify the kind of text and the recipient.** Fill in: who reads it, what relationship exists already, what the text must do (tell, explain, ask, invite, reassure, take a position), and which parts come from the person and which are new.
2. **Read the style file:** `[path to the style file]`.
3. **Pick two or three real samples of the same kind** from `[path to the samples folder]`. A sample matches first on recipient and kind of text, then on intention. If there is no exact match, combine one sample with the same recipient and one with the same kind, and say what is missing. Read the passages, not only the folder labels. Say which samples you used.
4. **Draft.** First write in a few lines what the text must do, then write it. Facts, figures, dates and promises come from the person or the sources given. If a detail is missing, leave a placeholder or ask.
5. **Pass against generic phrasing.** Reread the draft next to one sample and check:
   - Is the relationship with the reader the right one?
   - Are there phrases that could appear in anyone's text (stock openings, stock closings, empty intensifiers)?
   - Did a habit from another kind of text slip in?
   - Did you invent an experience, a feeling, a certainty or a promise?
   - Are the sentences connected, or isolated and clipped?
6. **Protect the person's own sentences.** Mark each passage as: written by the person (do not rewrite), approved by the person (valid reference, do not change without a reason), generated (can be rewritten), unknown or someone else's (never evidence of the voice). A mixed draft gets a mark per passage, not one label.
7. **Record corrections.** When the person changes a draft, keep the before and after and add a case to `[path to the learning log]`: date, kind of text, what you wrote, what they changed, the rule learned and its scope. Do not turn it into a permanent rule on your own.

## Rules that hold in every case

- Real samples come before descriptions. If the style file and a sample disagree, show the person the difference and ask.
- Judge "does it sound like them" separately from "is it effective". Do not add hooks, calls to action or polish that were not asked for.
- A correction learned on one text stays local until it repeats in independent contexts or the person says it is general.
- The style file changes only after the person confirms directly. Propose the change as before and after text.
- Relative time words ("tonight", "tomorrow") are recalculated from today's date each time. Never copied from an earlier message.
